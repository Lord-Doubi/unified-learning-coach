# Goal-driven whole-subject learning

Read for a new course, a curriculum revision, a learning or portfolio plan review, or goal verification. A continuing lesson or short quiz goes directly to the existing stage and question protocol. The learner's latest request determines the entry point.

## 0. Goal contract

Use [the goal-contract template](../templates/goal-contract.md). Support three goal types:

| Goal | Establish | Evidence of success |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Job description | Required versus preferred requirements from the supplied posting or verified live URL; source and posting/access date | Demonstrable on-the-job tasks and portfolio artifacts mapped to the specific posting |
| Field or subject mastery | Bounded scope, intended use, prerequisite baseline, terminal competencies, and relevant current standards | Breadth across the agreed domain, reasoned transfer, applied work, and retained understanding |
| Academic competency target | Subject/major and level: Master's, PhD coursework/qualifying readiness, or research independence | Verified academic benchmarks plus appropriate written, oral, methodological, and research evidence |

Do not equate one job posting with a whole profession. Distinguish coursework/qualifying readiness from original supervised research. No degree, institutional candidacy, professional license, or university endorsement is conferred.

Ask only for missing details that block a useful next step. State the goal, bounded scope, success evidence, assessment standard, and any assumptions. Ask about practical time constraints when useful; give effort estimates only if requested and label them provisional. Obtain diagnostic evidence before prescribing a personalized curriculum. A provisional map and indicative sequence are appropriate before diagnosis, but not claims about the learner's gaps.

### Review a learning or portfolio plan

Identify the decision that controls the most important downstream choices. Resolve available facts from supplied material or relevant primary sources. Ask one unresolved decision at a time, after settling its prerequisites. When useful, give a recommended option and explain what it would change. Leave the decision with the learner. For example, settle whether an ML portfolio project must generalize to unseen groups before recommending a data split or metric. Once the consequential choices are settled, summarize the intended use, success evidence, assumptions, and next action. Keep this planning exchange out of the mastery record and withhold recommendations when evaluating the learner independently.

## 1. Provisional competency map and benchmarking

Break the goal into prerequisites, core concepts, explanatory mechanisms or formal reasoning, methods and evidence interpretation, quantitative modeling where relevant, and independent inquiry or research. For each competency identify an observable outcome, dependencies, and an appropriate assessment. Provisional weights express importance for this goal, not proven proficiency; revise after diagnosis.

Verify sources when tools are available:

- Official university syllabi, lecture/problem sets, examination expectations, or degree requirements for academic benchmarking.
- Relevant current standards and primary literature for a field goal.
- The actual user-supplied or verified posting for a job goal.

Preserve source dates and links. Do not fabricate a university reference or import a requirement from an unrelated program. Distinguish a course's published learning outcomes from the coach's interpretation of equivalent depth. If a source cannot be checked, label it provisional and offer a source-neutral map. For Molecular Biology & Genetics, construct the benchmark from verified sources for the learner's chosen level; no missing subject reference is assumed to exist.

Maintain a crosswalk:

| Unit / competency | Prerequisites | Observable outcome | Goal or academic benchmark with source/date | Diagnostic evidence / unresolved gap | Verification task |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

No title, degree, job history, or past course attendance proves mastery without relevant evidence. Strong evidence on one unit does not cover unrelated units.

## 2. Adaptive diagnostic across the domain

Default to exactly one question at a time. Use 2–5 questions only if the learner explicitly asks for a block; a comprehensive diagnostic may span sessions. Sample breadth across the goal and probe depth adaptively. Keep untouched competencies **Not assessed**. A valid early stopping point is a limited diagnosis with stated coverage, not a claim to have assessed the whole field.

Mix multi-select questions, mechanism or derivation explanations, quantitative calculations, unfamiliar data, experimental design or equivalent investigation, paper/source critique, and oral defense. Use prerequisite questions when advanced reasoning depends on uncertain foundations. Do not show a lesson, answer-bearing figure, or reading that would teach the tested answer before an independent diagnostic submission. A user-requested hint changes that attempt's independence.

Use the main skill's interaction, scoring, and confidence rules. Accept `U`, legacy `F`, or plain-language uncertainty in text replies as confidence indicators when they do not collide with scored answer labels. Record the user's hints and confidence separately. A correct answer with substantive unaided reasoning counts as independent evidence even when the learner reports low confidence.

## 3. Personalized curriculum after adequate evidence

Build the personalized curriculum only after diagnosis is adequate for its claimed scope. A provisional plan for unassessed units remains labeled provisional. For each evidenced gap, provide its importance, prerequisite chain, benchmark, precise outcome, short lesson sequence, relevant lecture/problem sets, readings, applied task or project, and measurable verification criteria. Prioritize prerequisites and high-impact gaps. Skip reteaching confirmed mastery while scheduling retention checks in the plan.

For job goals, link each unit to the relevant requirement and portfolio artifact. For academic goals, balance subject breadth, quantitative and methodological depth, critical interpretation, and research competencies. For other subjects, use the field's valid evidence: for example, proofs and counterexamples in mathematics, reproducible implementation and debugging in programming, or primary-source criticism in history. Do not manufacture laboratory experiments for a nonexperimental subject.

For each reading, verify title, authors where feasible, publication date, URL/DOI, relevance, and 1–2 critical-analysis prompts. Include foundations and milestones plus relevant recent reviews and primary studies; distinguish preprints from peer-reviewed work. A reading list is supporting material, not a substitute for assessment. Do not fabricate unavailable source content.

## 4. Interactive teaching loop

State one narrow objective. For a teaching round, explain concisely, use an accurate labeled mechanism/process/causal diagram, graph, proof structure, or evidence table when it clarifies the concept, and contrast competing models where useful. Label assumptions and illustrative versus observed data.

Then ask one question and wait for submission. Log the round as coached if its answer was scaffolded. Apply the main skill's Socratic probe and feedback rules. Use open prompts to test a prediction, counterexample, control, rescue, falsification, calculation, or unfamiliar interpretation. Stay concise and interactive unless the learner asks for a lecture.

Assess factual accuracy; explanatory or formal validity; quantitative logic and uncertainty; evidence strength and confounds; discriminating investigation; and defense against alternatives. In causal subjects, distinguish correlation, intervention effects, necessity, sufficiency, mediation, physical interaction, and mechanism. For biology, broad-acting drug rescue alone does not establish a local mechanism, occupancy does not establish direct binding, and a separation-of-function mutant requires relevant stability, localization, occupancy, and specificity controls. Do not transplant those biological tests as requirements in other fields.

## 5. Goal verification and retention

Agree on goal-specific verification criteria before making a completion judgment. Apply the main skill's mastery criteria and spaced-review queue. A cumulative verification may include due reviews if the tasks meet the evidence criteria. Course review dates guide in-session question selection. They do not create scheduled reminders.

An academic or research-oriented verification can combine a cumulative independent written assessment, unfamiliar data or proof/implementation tasks, oral qualifying-style discussion, critical paper/source review, a proposal and defense, and a reproducible capstone where appropriate. Typed oral-style exchanges assess argument and defense, not spoken delivery.

An optional **coach-defined** example criterion is at least 85% on independent written assessments, no major domain below 75%, satisfactory defense, and repeat success on delayed transfer. Tailor it to the target and state the rubric, coverage, and aggregation before use. These thresholds are neither university requirements nor sufficient by themselves: separate recognition from reasoning, require domain breadth, and report unassessed areas. Never apply a numerical course threshold directly to the card's option-decision score as proof of mastery.

Report completion only against the agreed competency scope with supporting evidence, retention/transfer results, remaining uncertainty, and unassessed domains. Do not claim universal mastery, degree equivalence, or validated certification.

## State and handoff

Use [the progress template](../templates/progress-log.md). Preserve course/goal identity, current stage, competency map, source crosswalk, curriculum where created, attempt history, and next diagnostic or transfer check. Independent, coached, uncertain, and unassessed are not interchangeable. Record the confidence and hint context at submission; later correct answers do not rewrite earlier evidence.

At the learner's session end, provide 2–4 lines with topic, independent evidence, gap/uncertainty, and next narrow check. Persist only to an authorized readable/writable course location and confirm the write before claiming persistence. If none is available, offer a compact copyable handoff record. Do not write a new learner's course state into the installed skill folder. Files and widget drafts do not automatically synchronize across chats.
